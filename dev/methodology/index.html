Methodology

This page describes how the France plugin turns the raw inventory into the indicators returned by process_data. All estimates are computed by year and department and then summed to regions and the country; ratios are always recomputed from the summed totals.

Data

SourceContent used
IGN inventory, PLACETTE.csvplots: campaign, identifier, coordinates (Lambert-93), visit (1st or 2nd), department, land cover class
IGN inventory, ARBRE.csvtrees: species code, status at the 1st and 2nd visit, circumference, height, volume, weight, 5-year radial increment, age at 1.30 m
IGN inventory, espar-cdref13.csvspecies codes → Latin names (TAXREF v13)
Corine Land Cover 2006forest classes 311 (broadleaved), 312 (coniferous), 313 (mixed)
france-geojsonsimplified borders of the country, regions and departments

Inventory plots are kept when their land cover class is forest (closed or open forest, groves, poplar plantations).

Forest area

The polygons of the three forest classes are assigned to departments by the centroid of their largest ring, and their areas are summed. Areas are exact because Corine Land Cover is distributed in an equal-area projection (ETRS89-LAEA). The resulting forest area (about 14 Mha) is smaller than the IGN estimate (about 17 Mha), mainly because the land cover map does not include open forests and transitional woodland; all the totals of the package are expressed on this land cover basis.

The area of a species (ha) is the forest area multiplied by the share of the species in the basal area of the department, so that species areas add up to the forest area.

Growing stock

For each plot at its first visit, the volume per hectare of a species is the sum over its living trees of V × W (tree volume × weight, i.e. the number of trees per hectare that the tree represents). Plot values are averaged over all the forest plots of the department (plots without the species count as zero) and multiplied by the forest area:

\[\mathrm{vol}_{d,s} = A_d \cdot \frac{1}{n_d} \sum_{p \in d} \sum_{i \in p,\, s} V_i W_i\]

Biological growth

The 5-year radial increment IR5 gives the diameter five years earlier, $D_5 = D - 2\,IR5$. Assuming $V \propto D^{\beta}$, the annual relative volume growth of a tree is

\[g = \frac{1 - (D_5 / D)^{\beta}}{5}\]

where $\beta$ is estimated for each species by a log-log regression of volume on diameter. Since 2014 the increment is measured on a subsample of trees: the other trees get the mean g of their campaign, species and 10-cm diameter class. Biological growth is then estimated like the growing stock, from $g\,V W$. The recruitment of trees crossing the 7.5 cm threshold is not included, so growth is slightly underestimated.

Harvest and mortality

Plots are revisited five years after their first visit and the status of each tree is recorded. Trees found cut (codes 6, 7) give the harvest; trees found dead, broken or windthrown (codes 5, C, M, A, 1, 2, T) give the natural mortality. Their volume at the first visit, divided by five, is an annual flow attributed to the campaign of the second visit, and estimated per hectare over all the revisited plots of the department.

Between 2010 and 2014 the second visit followed a lighter protocol (trees were re-assessed on a fraction of the plots only), so harvest and mortality are available from 2015.

  • mortality_pc = 100 × mortality_volumes / vol (% of the growing stock per year);
  • share_bio_growth_harvested = harvested_volumes / biological_growth.

Sampling error and pooling

A department has about 50–150 forest plots per campaign, and a few dozen revisited plots. Single-year estimates for small areas, minor species and flows are therefore noisy. The window argument of process_data pools consecutive campaigns (the official IGN results use 5-year windows).

Growth models

estimate_growth fits the Chapman-Richards function

\[y(t) = a \left(1 - e^{-b t}\right)^{c}, \qquad c \ge 1\]

by nonlinear least squares (LsqFit.jl) on the living trees whose age at 1.30 m was measured (about 10 000 trees per year since 2006). y is the tree volume (default), the diameter or the height. Starting values come from a grid search on b and c with a solved by linear least squares. When the observed ages do not reach the asymptote (young plantations, slow-growing species), a is poorly identified and the curve should only be read within the observed ages.

Report

create_report uses the indicators of the selected scope. A variable is flagged as highly variable when its mean absolute year-on-year change exceeds cv_threshold (5 %) of its mean level; for those variables, the report compares the year with the previous year and with the mean of the five previous years.
